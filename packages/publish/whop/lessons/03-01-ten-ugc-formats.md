# Ten UGC formats

A format is not a prompt. "Make an ASMR unboxing" tells a model almost nothing. A format is a
contract about what proof the viewer gets, what physical action carries it, and where the ask
lands. This lesson turns ten recurring forms into that contract.

## Why format-first beats prompt-first

Short-form ads fail in predictable ways: the hook does not interrupt, the product never gets
demonstrated, or the ask arrives before the viewer has a reason to care. Choosing a format
fixes the *skeleton* — proof type, action, pacing, CTA placement — before any generation
happens. Then the model only has to render, not to invent structure.

For every format below, fill in three fields:

- **Proof** — what makes the claim believable on screen.
- **Visual action** — the specific physical thing hands and body do.
- **CTA** — the ask, and the second it lands.

## The ten forms

**1. Creator review.** Proof: a person's stated experience over time. Action: handling,
wearing, comparing. Best for products whose value is subjective. Weakest on hard specs.

**2. ASMR unboxing.** Proof: material quality, heard rather than argued. Action: peeling film,
lifting lids, sliding trays, close hand movement at slow, deliberate speed. Sound design is
the deliverable, not a garnish.

**3. Restocking ASMR.** Proof: abundance and order. Action: repetitive placement, alignment,
filling. Sells satisfaction more than the product; works for consumables and multiples.

**4. Before and after.** Proof: a visible state change. Action: the transition itself, in one
unbroken move if possible. The most persuasive and the most legally dangerous — a before and
after *is* a performance claim. Substantiate it or do not run it.

**5. Torture test.** Proof: survival under abuse. Action: drop, bend, submerge, scratch. Only
run this if the product actually survives, in the condition a customer would buy.

**6. Discovery footage.** Proof: apparent candidness — pet cam, security cam, found angle.
Action: minimal and un-performed. Cheap to produce, high stop rate, easy to overuse.

**7. Street reveal.** Proof: a stranger's unscripted reaction. Action: the reveal beat.
Requires real consent from anyone identifiable, which is the part people skip.

**8. Cinematic macro.** Proof: craftsmanship, shown at a scale the eye cannot reach unaided.
Action: slow camera travel across surface and mechanism. Strong for materials and hardware.

**9. Fantasy commercial.** Proof: none — it trades on aspiration. Action: stylised, impossible
motion. Use for brand, not for conversion, and never to imply a capability.

**10. Absurd spectacle.** Proof: none, deliberately. Action: escalation past plausibility.
High ceiling on reach, low floor on trust. Keep it away from any factual claim.

## Structure inside the format

Regardless of form, the beats are:

1. **Hook** — the first beat interrupts the scroll. It earns the next two seconds.
2. **Product interaction** — hands on the thing, unambiguously.
3. **Proof** — the format's chosen evidence.
4. **Objection** — name the doubt the viewer already has and answer it.
5. **CTA** — one ask, specific, at a moment the viewer has a reason to act.

Cut anything that is not one of these five.

## Pattern interruption without dishonesty

A hook works by breaking expectation. It does not have to break trust. Provocation that
promises something the product cannot do buys attention and spends credibility, and on a
product with a real feature set that trade is simply bad business. Interrupt with an unexpected
*true* thing.

## Hands are the tell

The fastest way to spot synthetic UGC is hand and product interaction — grip that does not
match weight, fingers that pass through a surface, a device held at an angle no one holds. When
you write the shot, specify which hand, what it contacts, and how the object responds. Then
review generated output specifically for it. This is the highest-value review pass in the whole
commercial pipeline.

## Deliverable

Three formats chosen for your product, each filled in with proof, visual action and CTA, and
each broken into the five beats with a target duration per beat.
