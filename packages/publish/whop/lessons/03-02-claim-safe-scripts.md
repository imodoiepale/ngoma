# Claim-safe scripts

This lesson exists because the fastest way to lose an ad account, a supplier relationship or a
court case is a sentence that took four seconds to write.

## The claim sheet

Before a script is approved, every factual assertion in it goes in a table:

| Claim | Type | Source | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| "47-hour battery" | Performance | Manufacturer spec sheet, page/date | Verify |
| "12-minute recording" | Performance | Own test, dated | Confirmed |
| "KES 9,500" | Price | Your own price list | Confirmed |
| "Free delivery countrywide" | Offer | Your own terms | Confirmed |
| "Better than [brand]" | Comparative | — | Do not run |

A line does not go into a voice recording until its row reads Confirmed. Not "probably true",
not "the competitor says it too".

## Where bad claims come from

Almost always from a competitor's ad. Studying reference ads is core to this method, and
reference ads are *full* of unverified numbers, because the person who made them was selling,
not documenting. When you tear down a reference, the numbers in it are **evidence about their
marketing**, not evidence about your product. Carry across the structure. Leave the specs
behind.

If your own product genuinely has the spec, source it from your own supplier documentation and
put that in the sheet.

## Claim types, ranked by risk

**Performance claims.** Battery life, range, capacity, speed, durability. Need a source, and
the test conditions matter — a figure measured standing still is not the figure a walking
customer gets.

**Comparative claims.** "Better than", "beats", "same as the expensive one". Highest risk, and
they invite a response from a company with more lawyers than you. Avoid naming competitors.
Describe what yours does.

**Price and availability.** Cheap to get right and embarrassing to get wrong. Currency,
market, and whether the price includes delivery. Re-check before every reissue, because prices
move and old creative keeps circulating.

**Scarcity and urgency.** "Almost sold out", "sale ends tonight". Only if true. Standing
false urgency is a deceptive practice in most markets, and audiences learn to discount it
anyway.

**Capability claims about AI features.** Especially prone to overreach — translation quality,
recognition accuracy, assistant reliability. Demonstrate the actual behaviour rather than
describing an ideal.

**Health, safety, financial and legal outcomes.** Treat as out of bounds unless you have
formal substantiation and, where relevant, regulatory approval.

## Lines to refuse outright

- Anything implying the product helps someone cheat, defraud, or covertly record people who
  have not consented. Beyond the legal exposure, it attracts exactly the audience that will
  not buy and will get the account reported.
- Any suggestion of surveilling a specific person.
- Any implied endorsement — a brand, a celebrity, an authority — you do not have in writing.
- Any before/after implying a result the product does not reliably produce.

Discreet recording hardware deserves a specific note. A "nobody can tell you are filming"
angle is a compliance problem in most jurisdictions and a platform-policy problem on top. Sell
the hands-free convenience, the capture quality, the battery, the fit. Not the concealment.

## Disclosure

- Synthetic performer, synthetic voice, or AI-generated footage: disclose it where the
  platform requires it, and label the asset internally regardless so a later reviewer is not
  guessing.
- Paid partnership: disclose it.
- Dramatisation or simulated results: label on screen, in the same language as the ad.

## Review gate

A script leaves this stage only with:

1. A claim sheet where every row is Confirmed or the line is cut.
2. A named person who approved it.
3. A dated version number, so the approved wording is the wording that gets recorded.

Change a word after approval and the version increments. This is how you avoid recording a
voice track against a script nobody signed off.

## Deliverable

One script with a complete claim sheet, every row sourced, and a written note listing what you
removed from the reference ad and why.
